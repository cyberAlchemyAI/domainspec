Cross-Tree Mirroring for LLM Coercion

From DSPedia, the DomainSpec encyclopedia. (Redirected from Three-sibling structure.)

Cross-tree mirroring is a proposed structural prescription for DomainSpec repositories in which the L₁ (knowledge) tree and L₂ (implementation) tree share an identical apps/X/features/Y/ grammar, with a third sibling tree (/research) hosting pre-structural work. The prescription is anchored in the engineering question that originated the framework: can I force an LLM to write the right code?

The proposal extends folder-structure-fractal, which addressed vault-internal layout only, to the cross-tree case between knowledge and code. It introduces two distinct operations — promotion (taking residue into L₁) and compilation (taking L₁ into L₂) — and a path-coherence validator that enforces the mirror invariant mechanically.

1. Etymology and origin

The term cross-tree mirroring denotes the symmetric relationship between two file trees joined by a structural functor. It contrasts with folder-structure-fractal, which used fractal to describe within-tree recursive self-similarity. The proposal was first articulated in a working session on 2026-05-18 in response to a brief at maestro-trama/docs/repo-mirroring-problem.md.

2. Structure

The proposal mandates three top-level sibling directories. The two structured siblings (/domain_knowledge, /implementation) share the feature grammar. The unstructured sibling (/research) hosts residue — work-in-progress that has not yet earned an (app, feature) identity.

2.1 /research

The residue holding pen. Forcing pre-structural work into the structured grammar would invent identity rather than earn it. The theorem (see reflection-tower) admits only two horizontal layers; /research sits beside them as a sibling, not above them as a third layer.

2.2 /domain_knowledge

The L₁ tree. Specs, discoveries, domain models, invariants. Every artifact has a known (app, feature) identity and lives at apps/X/features/Y/{aspect}.

2.3 /implementation

The L₂ tree. Code, tests, ops. Mirrored under the same grammar. For any path /implementation/apps/X/features/Y/..., the L₁ counterpart /domain_knowledge/apps/X/features/Y/ is computable by prefix substitution.

3. Promotion

Promotion is the operation moving a residue artifact from /research into /domain_knowledge/apps/X/features/Y/. Promotion is an identity change in place — distinct from compilation, which generates a new artifact in the mirror tree.

Promotion conditions are binary: identity-earned + manual greenlight. Stability, citation pull, and confidence thresholds are observational signals, not gates.

4. Compilation

Compilation is the application of the functor Δ: L₁ → L₂, a left Kan extension defined in DomainSpec.lean. In the present discovery the compilation gate is operationalized as a structural-completeness validator; the formal T0' obligations are deferred as a long-term aspiration.

5. Validator

The path-coherence validator extends the layer: mechanism of vault-folder-structure-constitution to the cross-tree case. It is implemented as a pre-commit hook and CI gate. Rejects any L₂ path with no L₁ counterpart (unless IMPLEMENTS.md exists), any L₁ spec without IMPLEMENTED-BY.md or spec-only status, and any non-research path that does not parse as (layer, app, feature, aspect).

7. Criticism

The adversarial review (Lens 04) raised seven attacks; five landed materially. The "coercion" warrant was demoted to "navigational signal + validator enforcement," recoverable only after a measurement protocol is established (see OQ-1). Multi-implementation cases require manifest indirection rather than a strict bijection. Scope is narrowed to /domainspec and maestro-trama-shaped repos; /house_project, /financas_pessoais, /football-stats-oracle require separate discoveries.
