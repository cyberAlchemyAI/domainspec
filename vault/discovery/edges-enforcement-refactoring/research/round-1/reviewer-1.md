# Reviewer 1 (Round 1) — Coherence with Constitution & Migration Phases

> Independent skeptic pass on `explorer.md`, scoped to **coherence with the existing constitution and migration phases**. Not evaluating operational viability (Reviewer 2 owns that). Authored without seeing reviewer-2.md.

---

## Verdict

**accept-with-revisions** — the proposal is coherent in *spirit* with the existing edge regime (catalog, carve-outs, bidirectionality intent, acyclicity, frontmatter ownership) and the constitutional surface it proposes to amend is correctly scoped. But the explorer (a) misnames the source-of-truth ontology in two places (`ontology-conventions.md` does not contain "Authority Rule 1" or "Authority Rules" — that vocabulary is invented), (b) miscounts the edge catalog (says "21 forward edges" four times; the constitution Appendix C says "**22 forward edges**"), and (c) does not address the `frontmatter-ownership-constitution.md` v1.0.0 carve-outs clause (§Rule 6) which already declares where carve-outs live (in `vault_common.frontmatter.carveouts`, not in narrative §8 prose), creating a quiet collision with the proposal's "carve-outs continue to be encoded in derivation logic." Each is fixable in Round 2 without re-opening the design.

The proposal does **not** violate the Edge Catalog (no edges added/renamed/removed), does **not** violate the acyclicity constitution (the derivation pipeline feeds the same edge graph), does **not** violate the frontmatter-ownership constitution's single-owner schema (it touches a body section, not frontmatter), and does **not** violate the discovery-structure constitution's shape rules in a load-bearing way (one structural deviation noted in Finding 6, minor).

---

## Findings

### 1. [block] Phantom "Authority Rule 1" / "Authority Rules" vocabulary
- **Location:** Explorer §Objective ("Victor's 'Authority Rule 1'"); also in the routing brief that motivates the discovery.
- **Claim:** The explorer asserts that `ontology-conventions.md` treats the `## Connections` table as "Victor's 'Authority Rule 1'."
- **Evidence:** `ontology-conventions.md` v2.3.0 contains **no occurrence of the phrase "Authority Rule"** (verified across §1–§14 + Appendices A–D). The closest binding statement is §8 "Directionality Principle": *"Edges between vault nodes must be declared on both endpoints. ... Both sides are written explicitly in Markdown — there is no SQL-layer inference."* This binds **bidirectionality** as the load-bearing rule, not "the table is authoritative." Section 8 plus Appendix C §"Authoring rules" do say the `## Connections` block is the **declared surface**, but they do not name it as Authority Rule 1, and they do not exclude inline links from being a co-equal declaration surface.
- **Why this matters:** The proposal's framing — "flip Authority Rule 1" — overstates the constraint it is inverting. The actual constraint is weaker: §8 says "declared on both endpoints" and Appendix C §1 says "a `## Connections` block on the source declares the forward edge." Neither forbids body-link declarations; the table is currently the **only** declaration surface only by convention, not by named rule. The proposal can be defended without inventing a rule name.
- **Recommendation:** Strike the phrase "Authority Rule 1" in §Objective and the routing brief. Replace with: *"Today the `## Connections` table is the de-facto authoritative declared surface (per `ontology-conventions.md` §8 + Appendix C Authoring Rule 1: 'A `## Connections` block on the source declares the forward edge'); body-prose links are not treated as edge declarations."* This is a true citation; the current text invokes an authority that does not exist.

### 2. [block] Edge-catalog count off-by-one (21 vs 22)
- **Location:** Explorer §1 ("What stays the same") bullet 1: "The 21-edge catalog (`ontology-conventions.md` Appendix C)"; §3.3 Deprecations: "The 21-edge catalog (Appendix C) — same names, same directionality, same inverses"; §3.4 first bullet; §D-3 of Decision Summary.
- **Claim:** Catalog has 21 forward edges.
- **Evidence:** `ontology-conventions.md` Appendix C opening sentence: *"The vault has **22 forward edges** organized into three semantically distinct categories."* Manual count of the three subtables: Epistemic (15: `derives-from`, `supersedes`, `contradicts`, `codified-as`, `operationalized-by`, `implements`, `validates`, `refines`, `governed-by`, `subclass-of`, `part-of`, `alternative-to`, `synthesized-by`, `corroborates`, `retrofits`), Provenance (9: `continues-from`, `creates`, `modifies`, `revisits`, `refutes`, `surfaces-conflict`, `opens-question`, `closes-question`, `consumes`), Reference (1: `cites`) — **25 by my count**, which suggests the constitution itself has a stale count and the explorer inherited it from a still-older snapshot. Regardless, "21" appears nowhere in the current `ontology-conventions.md`.
- **Why this matters:** Both the discovery and the constitution it cites disagree on a number that is supposed to be the load-bearing invariant ("the catalog is preserved unchanged"). If the next-round audit reads the explorer literally and finds 25 (or 22) edges in the catalog, it will flag a contradiction the proposal did not intend.
- **Recommendation:** Replace "21-edge catalog" with "Appendix C edge catalog (currently declared as 22 forward edges; the actual catalog table enumeration may require an independent reconciliation pass — out of scope here)." This both fixes the number and surfaces a real residue: the constitution and its own claim disagree.

### 3. [major] Migration-phase coherence — proposal sits outside the constitution's promotion path it implicitly requires
- **Location:** Explorer §3 "The Constitutional Change" (proposes to amend `ontology-conventions.md` §8); also §6 OQ-9 (sequencing of constitution amendment vs tool existence).
- **Claim:** The proposal authors itself as a `discovery` and proposes a §8 amendment.
- **Evidence:** `discovery-structure-constitution.md` §6 "Promotion path" states: *"A discovery does not promote itself. It surfaces *candidates*: ... Constitution candidates — working principles the discovery argues for. ... Actually creating those files is a separate, deliberate act."* The explorer's §3.2 ("New subsection: 'Typed body links — authoring rules'") and §3.1 (replacement sentence for §8) are written as **drafted constitutional text**, not as constitution *candidates*. This crosses the discovery-vs-constitution boundary the structure constitution explicitly polices.
- **Why this matters:** A discovery that drafts its own constitutional amendment text bypasses the "deliberate act" boundary in `discovery-structure-constitution.md` §6 and creates a precedent where a single discovery can ship constitutional language without the discovery → premise → constitution chain being walked. The `frontmatter-ownership-constitution.md` §Rule 7 reinforces this: *"When this constitution is amended, the version bump is recorded in `last_updated` and a session note records the rationale."* — implying constitutional amendments are a session-event, not a discovery output.
- **Recommendation:** Demote §3.1 and §3.2 from "drafted text the constitution would adopt" to "constitutional amendment candidate — sketch of the §8 surface the discovery would propose, pending promotion through `discovery-structure-constitution.md` §6." Add an explicit Next Moves entry: "Draft a separate constitution-amendment node (or session) once Rounds 2–3 close, per `discovery-structure-constitution.md` §6." This preserves the design content while respecting the promotion boundary.

### 4. [major] Edge-catalog conformance — proposal does not say what happens to `retrofits` (the forward-only-with-no-inverse edge) or to `contradicts` self-loops under the derivation
- **Location:** Explorer §2 "The derivation pipeline" steps 3–5; OQ-2 ("`contradicts` symmetry under typed body links").
- **Claim:** The pipeline emits forward edges and projects inverses, with carve-outs for skill/agent targets and session sources.
- **Evidence:** Appendix C's Epistemic table contains **`retrofits`** with the explicit note: *"`retrofits` intentionally lacks an inverse. If query patterns require 'what did this node retrofit onto,' trace via the parent `research.md`."* The proposal's step 5 ("project the inverse onto the target") has no carve-out for `retrofits` and would either (a) attempt to project an inverse that does not exist in the catalog (malformed output) or (b) silently drop the edge. `edge-acyclicity-constitution.md` §1 also names `retrofits` is implicitly outside the acyclic set table — the pipeline must know this too. Similarly, `contradicts` is symmetric (both sides use the same name); the proposal flags this as OQ-2 but does not commit to a derivation rule, which means the pipeline as written would emit a duplicate `contradicts` edge if both sides author it.
- **Why this matters:** A derivation pipeline that does not handle the catalog's edge-cases (forward-only `retrofits`, symmetric `contradicts`, the two §8 carve-outs the proposal *does* mention) is not "the 21-edge catalog preserved unchanged" — it is a pipeline that quietly drops or duplicates two of the catalog's 22 entries. The explorer's "what stays the same" claim that "the 21-edge catalog ... is preserved unchanged" is not yet sustained.
- **Recommendation:** Add a §3.5 "Edge-class-specific handling" naming: (i) `retrofits` is forward-only-by-design and the derivation must not project an inverse; (ii) `contradicts` is symmetric — pick one of {declared once + projected on the other side, declared on both ends + deduplicated} as the binding rule, even if provisional; (iii) the `subclass-of` tree-constraint (Appendix C: *"Tree, not DAG — multiple inheritance forbidden"*) is a derivation invariant that must be checked post-derivation. Until these three are named, "the catalog is preserved" overstates what the explorer shows.

### 5. [major] Connection Coverage Policy — the explorer's "prose vs. edge" rule is correct in intent but does not say what happens to existing non-`title`'d links that *are semantically* edges (the silent-edge problem)
- **Location:** Explorer §2 "Hyperlink-as-source-of-truth"; §3.2 "Prose links"; D-4.
- **Claim:** "A markdown link whose `title` is absent, empty, or not a member of the edge catalog is **not an edge** under this proposal. It is prose. This preserves Victor's existing posture that not every link is a typed graph claim."
- **Evidence:** This rule is internally consistent and matches the spirit of §8 (bidirectionality applies to *declared* edges only; decorative links are not declarations). However: the explorer's §1 "What's broken" itself catalogs "~20 inbound edges declared from elsewhere" landing on the three high-traffic sinks — many of which are **declared as table rows on the source side today** but exist as **un-titled body links** on the source as well. Under the proposed regime, those table rows are deleted (table becomes derived), and if the existing body link is not retroactively titled, **the edge silently disappears**. The proposal flags this as TD-1 (migration is out of scope) but does not flag it as a *correctness* issue for the cutover: the moment the constitution amends, every existing edge whose body-link was not also titled becomes prose. This is a silent edge-deletion event.
- **Why this matters:** Connection Coverage Policy in the constitution is implicit (no separate document, but §8 + Appendix C §Authoring Rule 1 jointly establish it). The proposal preserves the policy in steady state, but the cutover violates it transiently — and "transient" here means "until the migration pass runs," which TD-1 says is undesigned. OQ-9 acknowledges this as a sequencing concern but frames it as a *staleness* problem ("`## Connections` blocks become stale"), not as a *silent edge deletion* problem. They are different.
- **Recommendation:** Promote part of TD-1 + OQ-9 into a new OQ-10 explicitly named: *"Cutover correctness — between constitution amendment and migration completion, what prevents silent edge deletion?"* and propose a Round-2 candidate: the constitution amendment is gated on the migration pass having run at least once. This converts the silent-deletion risk into an explicit ordering invariant.

### 6. [major] Interaction with `inverse-edge-fix` — the explorer's OQ-6 names the question but the proposal's posture creates a constitutional contradiction that the question does not surface
- **Location:** Explorer §6 OQ-6; §3.3 "Deprecations" ("The `inverse-edge-fix` Tier 2 sweep ... becomes a one-time migration").
- **Claim:** The relationship between this proposal and `inverse-edge-fix` "needs to be named."
- **Evidence:** `inverse-edge-fix.md` §3.1 Tier 1 commits to adding `## Connections` blocks to the three sinks; §3.2 Tier 2 commits to hand-authoring inverse rows on every Category-4 target. **The hand-authoring is the load-bearing mechanism.** If `inverse-edge-fix` Tier 1+2 lands first, the corpus gains ~110 new hand-authored rows that this proposal would then immediately demote to "derived output, never hand-edited" (per D-2). That is not just operational friction — it inverts the deprecation direction within a span of weeks. Conversely, if this proposal lands first, `inverse-edge-fix` Tier 2's "hand-authoring is the discipline" becomes immediately false. The two are not just overlapping; under the current constitution they are **mutually contradictory in their authoring posture**, and only one can be "active" at a time.
- **Why this matters:** `discovery-structure-constitution.md` §7 says: *"No revision in place. Refinements happen in new discoveries that `supersedes:` the original."* The relationship between this discovery and `inverse-edge-fix` is not "cites" (as currently in the `## Connections` table) — if this proposal is adopted, this discovery **supersedes** `inverse-edge-fix` Tier 2 specifically. The current `## Connections` row says `cites`; the relationship is actually `supersedes` (at least partially, on the Tier-2 component).
- **Recommendation:** OQ-6 should be split into two: (a) "operational interaction" (the one currently posed), and (b) "epistemic relationship — does this discovery `supersede` `inverse-edge-fix` Tier 2 in the catalog sense?" Until (b) is answered, the `## Connections` row on `inverse-edge-fix` is mis-typed. If the Round-2 answer is "yes, supersede Tier 2," then `## Connections` for that edge changes from `cites` to `supersedes`, and `inverse-edge-fix` itself needs a session note recording the partial supersession.

### 7. [minor] Discovery-structure conformance — explorer.md is named `explorer.md` not `README.md`, lives under `research/round-1/`, and lacks required body sections
- **Location:** Entire file structure.
- **Claim:** This is a discovery (frontmatter says `node_type: discovery`).
- **Evidence:** `discovery-structure-constitution.md` §1 mandates: `vault/discovery/<slug>/README.md` + `lenses/`. §3 mandates body sections: Claim, Status, Summary, Lenses, Open Questions, Next Moves. The current file has §Objective, §1 Business Context, §2 Core Concepts, §3 Constitutional Change, §4 Decision Summary, §5 Tracked Debt, §6 Open Questions, §Connections — none of which are the required headers, and the file is `explorer.md` under `research/round-1/`, not `README.md` at the slug root.
- **Why this matters:** Mostly a layout/naming inconsistency, not a substantive defect. The file is functioning as a Round-1 research artifact under a multi-round dispatch, which is a reasonable use of the `research/` subfolder. But its frontmatter declares `node_type: discovery`, which collides with its actual epistemic role: it is `node_type: research` (per `ontology-conventions.md` Appendix B: *"Exploration and evidence-gathering produced by a single investigation (one agent, one pass, or one source). Feeds into a discovery; never authoritative on its own."*). The discovery — the consolidated synthesis citing this and the reviewer files — does not yet exist.
- **Recommendation:** Change frontmatter `node_type: discovery` → `node_type: research`. The eventual consolidated discovery (the Round-3 output, or a separate `README.md` at the slug root) is what carries `node_type: discovery` and the required sections. This also resolves the partial mismatch with `discovery-structure-constitution.md` §10: *"`research/` — exploration of evidence. Lenses, experiments, notes, audits. ... `node_type: research`."*

### 8. [minor] Subset rule — one inflated claim in §2
- **Location:** Explorer §2 "Why this is not 'just SQL-layer inference'" final sentence: *"The A-3 rejection was against runtime-only computation with no Markdown materialization; the proposal here materializes on write, not on read."*
- **Claim:** The A-3 rejection was specifically against runtime-only computation.
- **Evidence:** I don't have access to the verbatim A-3 text in `domainspec-vault-edges/discovery.md` here, but the explorer reconstructs A-3 from memory in the same paragraph (*"explicitly rejected 'inverse-via-SQL-computation rather than bidirectional Markdown authoring' on the grounds that bidirectional Markdown makes both endpoints discoverable by reading either file"*) — the cited rationale is "discoverable by reading either file," not "no runtime-only computation." The proposal's restatement ("against runtime-only computation with no Markdown materialization") narrows A-3's actual reason to a phrasing convenient for the proposal. The two are close but not identical; the original rationale is **local readability**, the restatement is **non-runtime materialization**. They have different failure modes.
- **Why this matters:** Minor subset-rule slip. The restatement makes A-3 sound like it admits the proposal's design when in fact A-3 was a broader rejection of "discoverability not local to the file."
- **Recommendation:** Quote A-3 verbatim from `domainspec-vault-edges/discovery.md` in Round 2; if A-3's stated rationale really is "no SQL-layer inference because local readability matters," then the proposal preserves it (the `## Connections` block is still locally readable post-regeneration). If A-3 was broader, the proposal needs to address the broader form.

### 9. [minor] Frontmatter-ownership-constitution interaction unaddressed
- **Location:** Explorer §3.4 "What does NOT change ... the frontmatter-ownership constitution"; §Connections row 6.
- **Claim:** "The derivation does NOT extend frontmatter; it rewrites a body section. The single-owner schema for frontmatter is untouched."
- **Evidence:** Technically correct. But `frontmatter-ownership-constitution.md` Rule 6 says: *"Existing carve-outs from `ontology-conventions.md` (sessions are forward-only on `is_session: true`; `.claude/skills/**` and `.claude/agents/**` are forward-only edge targets) live in `vault_common.frontmatter.carveouts` as explicit functions, not as scattered conditionals."* The proposal's §3.2 says "Carve-outs ... continue to suppress inverse generation" — *in the derivation pipeline*. This means there would be **two** sites for carve-outs: `vault_common.frontmatter.carveouts` (per Rule 6) and the derivation pipeline (new). Either the new pipeline calls into `vault_common.frontmatter.carveouts` (preserving single ownership) or it forks the logic (violating Rule 6 in spirit). The explorer does not address this.
- **Recommendation:** Add a sentence to §3.4: *"The derivation pipeline must consume carve-outs from `vault_common.frontmatter.carveouts` (per `frontmatter-ownership-constitution.md` Rule 6), not reimplement them. This preserves the single-owner constraint."* This is a 1-line fix that closes the gap.

### 10. [minor] OQ-1 (forward-only vs both-direction-allowed) silently re-opens a closed question
- **Location:** Explorer §6 OQ-1.
- **Claim:** "Round-1 does not pick" between (a) forward-only authoring and (b) either direction.
- **Evidence:** `ontology-conventions.md` Appendix C §Authoring Rule 1: *"A `## Connections` block on the source declares the forward edge; the target document declares the inverse. Both forms are in this catalog. **Asymmetric declarations between vault nodes are bugs.**"* The constitution already pins the rule: *both* sides declare, both use catalog names (forward on source, inverse on target). The proposal's OQ-1 frames this as open, when in the existing constitution it is closed. Under the proposal, the closed answer becomes "the body link on the source carries the forward name; the inverse appears on the target via derivation, also using its catalog name." That is consistent with the existing closure — OQ-1 should reflect that the catalog already commits to forward-on-source.
- **Recommendation:** Reframe OQ-1 as: *"The catalog already pins forward-on-source (Appendix C Authoring Rule 1). Under the proposal, does the body link author write the forward name (consistent with catalog) or may they write the inverse name and have the pipeline normalize? The catalog's rule strongly suggests forward-only authoring is the correct binding."* This converts an open question into a quasi-closed one with a defended Round-1 answer.

## Strongest Concern

**Finding 6 — the unstated `supersedes` relationship to `inverse-edge-fix`.** The other findings are corrections to a fundamentally sound design. Finding 6 names a load-bearing epistemic relationship the proposal mis-classifies. If this discovery's `## Connections` block declares `cites` toward `inverse-edge-fix` when the operational truth is `supersedes` (at least partially), then the vault gains a discovery whose stated relationship to its predecessor is false in the catalog sense. That is exactly the kind of drift the catalog exists to prevent. Worse, `discovery-structure-constitution.md` §7 ("No revision in place. Refinements happen in new discoveries that `supersedes:` the original") makes the supersession question constitutional, not optional. Until the explorer answers "does this discovery `supersede` `inverse-edge-fix` Tier 2 or not?", a downstream reader cannot tell whether to execute Tier 2 of `inverse-edge-fix` or to skip it. The two answers lead to wildly different vault states.

This is the one finding where a wrong answer doesn't just produce stale docs — it produces an authoring contradiction the constitution itself forbids.

## What the Explorer Got Right

- **Constitutional surgery is correctly minimal.** The proposal touches §8 of `ontology-conventions.md` and adds one subsection — it does not touch the 22-edge catalog, the acyclicity constitution, the frontmatter-ownership constitution, the carve-outs, or `contradicts`-as-symmetric. The surface area is exactly what the change requires.
- **Carve-outs are correctly preserved in scope.** §3.4 explicitly names skills/agents-forward-only-by-target and session-forward-only-by-source as untouched. The carve-out catalog in `ontology-conventions.md` §8 is honored, even if Finding 9 (single-site ownership per `frontmatter-ownership-constitution.md` Rule 6) needs a 1-line addition.
- **The `title` attribute choice is well-defended.** §2 "Why this carrier and not an alternative" rules out wikilinks, custom prefixes, fenced blocks, and YAML — each rejection cites a real constraint (CommonMark portability, visible-text pollution, tooling support). The choice is not arbitrary.
- **A-3 is addressed directly (modulo Finding 8).** §2 "Why this is not 'just SQL-layer inference'" engages the prior rejection rather than glossing it. The argument that the proposal preserves local-readability by materializing on write is structurally sound; only the restatement of A-3's rationale needs verbatim citation.
- **OQ-6 was the right question to surface,** even though Finding 6 says it didn't go far enough. Naming the in-flight discovery relationship is exactly what `discovery-structure-constitution.md` §7 demands; the gap is in the answer, not the framing of the question.
- **Migration is correctly out-of-scope per Victor's instruction, and TD-1 is honest about it.** The explorer does not pretend to design the cutover — it explicitly defers and flags the chicken-and-egg. This is the right Round-1 posture; Finding 5 (silent edge deletion) is a refinement on top of that honest deferral, not a reversal of it.
- **The §Connections authoring-note is the right kind of self-aware.** The note that "the discovery itself eats the dual-surface cost it proposes to eliminate" is exactly the kind of constraint-honesty the constitution rewards. Many discoveries would have hand-waved this; this one names it as transitional debt.
