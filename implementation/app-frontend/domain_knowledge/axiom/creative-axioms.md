# Creative Axioms

> Foundational commitments about how creatives behave in performance marketing. We take these as given. Revising one requires rethinking the Creative Operations area, the winning-creative classification, the removal rule, and how the team allocates production effort.

---

## Objective

This document defines the **non-negotiable truths about creatives** that justify the existence of the Creative Operations area and the shape of its core rules. It answers the question: *"What do we take as given about creatives, and what collapses if we challenge it?"*

These axioms are the bedrock. Every premise, constitution, and operational decision in `/domain_knowledge` ultimately derives from one or more of them. This list is still incomplete, we need to figure out the other axioms.

## AX-CRT-1 — Creatives produce extraordinary financial outcomes

A single winning creative can reshape the performance of an entire campaign — driving spend up, ROAS up, or exhausting a product's stock on its own. The distribution of creative outcomes is fat-tailed: most creatives contribute marginally, a few contribute disproportionately. Observed examples include stirrup-nao-marca, NoHo Socks, Boné Sixx, Tube Dress, and the Shantal organic collaboration that depleted stock.

This axiom justifies: the existence of the Creative Operations area, the financial investment in a production pipeline, the formal recognition of "Criativo Vencedor" as a tracked class, and the team's decision to measure creatives as first-class objects rather than as campaign inputs.

---

## AX-CRT-2 — Predicting which creative wins is really hard (maybe impossible?)

No amount of pre-production review, taste, or a-priori analysis reliably predicts which creative will win in campaign. The information to rank a creative is generated by the network — by impressions, clicks, and conversions — not by the team before the ad is live.

This axiom justifies: the volume bet (producing a large number of creatives to increase the probability of encountering winners), the removal rule (we cannot identify losers without exposure), and the rejection of "wait until the creative is perfect" as a strategy.

---

## AX-CRT-3 — Network trends can be exploited

Once a creative wins, the angle, format, or argument it used is not an accident — it is a signal about what the network (and its audience) is currently responsive to. Replicating that angle across other products and campaigns raises the probability that the next creative also wins. The stirrup-nao-marca angle generated 16+ derivative creatives across unrelated products; the pattern is explicit.

This axiom justifies: the Learning Loop, the practice of producing variations on proven angles, and the separation of "explore" (find new winners) from "exploit" (replicate proven winners).

---

## AX-CRT-4 — The network's interest aligns with the campaign objective

Meta, Google, and similar networks optimize delivery toward the campaign's stated objective (ROAS in our case). When the network increases a creative's spend share, it is because the network's internal model sees higher expected return from that creative. Spend share is therefore a usable proxy for "the network has seen signal here", and the absence of spend is a usable proxy for "the network has not seen signal".

This axiom justifies: the decision to use spend (and spend share specifically) as the primary input to the removal rule, rather than operator judgment or pre-production scoring.

> **Boundary condition:** this axiom is conditional on the campaign objective being set honestly and on the network not being adversarial. It can fail under bidding bugs, platform experiments, or campaigns with mis-set objectives — but those are exceptional states, not the default.
